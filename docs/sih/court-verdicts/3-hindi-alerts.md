# The verdict: GUILTY, 6 to 0

**The case:** Build Hindi and regional-language alerts and UI as a headline feature of ApprovalTrack for the SIH 2026 national round.

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## How the jury voted

| Juror | Vote | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Designer | GUILTY | A stranger looking at an AICTE approval-tracking product should see "nobody loses a deadline or forgets who owns the next step" in five seconds, and putting a Hindi toggle in the headline muddies that story into "it's a translated app" without anyone even having shown that language is why things slip. |
| The Retired Judge | GUILTY | A headline feature has to rest on proof, and the only support for this one is the team's own claim that coordinators are "often more comfortable in Hindi," which the defense itself admits has no interview, survey, count or source behind it. |
| The Competitor | GUILTY | If I were a rival team, I wouldn't bother fighting a Hindi toggle: I'd copy it in a day by translating my own strings, then beat Infera by demoing the modules their own deck promises and they haven't built, so as a headline it buys them nothing I can't match. |
| The Statistician | GUILTY | A headline feature built on a need with a sample size of zero, shipped while six pieces of the promised "8 module" platform are still unbuilt and the time left is unknown, is betting the "extraordinary" slot on an untested assumption, and both sides admit the record contains no data for it. |
| The Teenager | GUILTY | A language toggle looks cool for about ten seconds, and then the judges are staring at a deck promising 8 modules with six still missing, so nobody walks out talking about the Hindi, they talk about what isn't there. |
| The First-Time Customer | GUILTY | Seeing this for the first time, a Hindi toggle would not make me believe ApprovalTrack fixes my slipping deadlines, especially when the deck promises six modules I can't use yet, and nobody has shown me a single coordinator who asked for it. |

# Judgment

## Charges that stuck

1. Six promised modules remain unbuilt while Hindi is made the headline. (6 of 6 jurors)
2. No interview, survey, count or source shows that coordinators miss English reminders. (5 of 6 jurors)
3. The record names phone, email, spreadsheets and unclear ownership as the causes of slips, so a language toggle looks cosmetic and fixes nothing. (4 of 6 jurors)

## What the defense got right

1. The feature rests on working reminder and escalation machinery, so it changes the language of existing messages rather than adding a new subsystem.
2. "Alert Management" is in the problem statement's title, and an unreadable reminder cannot stop a slip, so Hindi fits as support even if it does not fit as the headline.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. Interview or count named smaller-college coordinators to show that English alerts get missed. Start this week. (6 of 6 jurors)
2. Finish the six unbuilt modules, or give them a dated plan, before Hindi takes headline space. Confirm the national round date first. (5 of 6 jurors)
3. Make "alerts people actually read" the headline, with Hindi as support. (2 of 6 jurors)

## The sentence

Your engine is sound, but the headline is not yet earned, so do not lead with Hindi at nationals on current evidence. This week, ask a handful of smaller-college coordinators how they read English reminders, and find out the national round date. If they confirm the problem, finish or schedule the six modules, then show Hindi as support under "alerts people actually read." If they do not, keep Hindi out of the headline and out of the demo.
