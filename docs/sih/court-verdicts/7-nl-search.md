# The verdict: GUILTY, 4 to 2

**The case:** Build natural-language search for officers as a headline feature of ApprovalTrack for the SIH 2026 national round.

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## How the jury voted

| Juror | Vote | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Designer | NOT GUILTY | A stranger gets this in five seconds: type a sentence, see editable filter chips, then see the list, and that plain, checkable moment is exactly what a judge remembers, even if "headline" oversells a convenience feature. |
| The Retired Judge | NOT GUILTY | Every harm the prosecution describes is conditional on facts the record leaves blank (the model, the time left, the filter list), and unknowns are not proof of failure, even though the defense's claim that this deserves the headline slot is just as unproven. |
| The Competitor | GUILTY | As a rival I would copy a text-to-filter-chips box in an afternoon, so it can't be the thing that sets them apart, and calling it the headline while six promised modules are unbuilt is the weak spot I would go after. |
| The Statistician | GUILTY | Teams that add a new headline feature while most of their promised scope is still unbuilt usually arrive with a thin demo and a deck they cannot back up, and nothing in the record (no model named, no accuracy test, no listed filters, no time estimate, no evidence officers struggle with filters) gives a reason to expect this team to be an exception. |
| The Teenager | GUILTY | Honestly it's a search box that fills in filters you could click yourself, and every hackathon team has a "chat with your data" thing, so making it the headline just isn't exciting enough to win a national round. |
| The First-Time Customer | GUILTY | As someone seeing this for the first time, I would not pick a product because I can type a sentence instead of clicking filters that already work, and I would be annoyed to learn that the document tracking and dashboards I was promised were still missing. |

## Charges that stuck

1. The search is a convenience any team could copy: the filters already exist, and nothing shows officers struggle with them. (5 jurors leaned on this.)
2. Six promised modules are unbuilt, and the time left before the national round is not stated. (4 jurors.)
3. No AI model is named, so the offline, no-paid-services promise is unproven, and there is no accuracy test. (3 jurors.)

## What the defense got right

1. Officers see and edit the AI's filter chips before results load, so a misread query is visible, not silently wrong.
2. Access rules still apply through the existing layer, so no officer sees cases outside their region. The prosecution accepted this.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. Make search a small add-on, not the headline. Four jurors named this. This week, lead the demo with the overdue-cases view you already have.
2. Name a local, offline parser and measure it. Four jurors wanted this. This week, pick a rule-based or small local model and score it on fixed test queries.
3. Finish the promised modules first. Three jurors wanted this. This week, write down the days left and estimate each module.

## The sentence

The case fails on sequencing, not on the idea. Keep the parser local, since a hosted AI service would turn the two jurors who backed you. Write down your days left and module list this week. If the modules fit, bring search back as a tested, offline add-on. If they do not, drop it from the national pitch.
