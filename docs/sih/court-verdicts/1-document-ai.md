# The verdict: GUILTY, 5 to 1

**The case:** Build AI document date extraction with source highlighting as a headline feature of ApprovalTrack for the SIH 2026 national round.

**The charge:** The case fails as stated: it should not go ahead the way it is described.

## How the jury voted

| Juror | Vote | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The Designer | NOT GUILTY | A stranger gets this in five seconds: a lease PDF goes in, the expiry date lights up on the page, the officer clicks confirm, and the reminder clock starts, and that one visible chain shows why the product matters better than anything else ApprovalTrack has. |
| The Retired Judge | GUILTY | The defense asks me to accept a headline feature whose central engine is described only as "uses AI", and by its own concession that question has to be settled before the team commits, so what the record holds is hope, not proof that it can go ahead as described. |
| The Competitor | GUILTY | If I were the rival team, I would copy "AI reads the date and highlights it" in a weekend with any extraction model, so I'd only fear them if their headline ran end to end on their own working deadline engine; as described, the extraction engine is unnamed and the document module under it is unbuilt, so I'd wait for it to stumble live and beat them on the promised modules they're missing. |
| The Statistician | GUILTY | The sample behind this headline is zero: it has no named method, no accuracy figure and no test on any document, only synthetic data, and the time left is unknown, so I cannot estimate how often it works and I won't stake the centrepiece of a national round on a rate that has never been measured. |

| The First-Time Customer | GUILTY | As an officer seeing this for the first time, I'd still have to check and click every date myself, and nobody can tell me what the AI is, whether it runs offline, or how often it gets the date right, so I have no reason to pick it over typing one date into a reminder engine that already works. |

## Charges that stuck

1. The AI step is unnamed: the record gives no model, no OCR approach for scanned PDFs, no accuracy figure, and no proof it runs offline as the team requires. (5 jurors)
2. The payoff is small and easy to copy: it saves an officer typing one date, and other teams can build "AI reads a date" quickly. (3 jurors)
3. The base is unbuilt and the time left is unknown, so the headline has no proven foundation or deadline. (2 jurors)

## What the defense got right

1. The one-click confirmation keeps the team's rule: unconfirmed values never drive deadlines, so a misread date cannot start an escalation on its own.
2. The confirmed date feeds the existing 30/7/1 reminder and escalation engine, a real chain that answers the lapsing-document problem.

## Conditions for acquittal

1. Working offline extraction on scanned lease and fire-certificate PDFs, using a named free method, with the highlighted source page visible on the demo laptop. (All six jurors' conditions include this.)
2. A measured accuracy figure on a stated, documented test set that includes scanned PDFs. (Jurors 4 and 6.)
3. The full chain shown live: a confirmed date triggers the existing reminder and escalation on screen. (Jurors 3 and 5.)

## The sentence

I find the idea has merit but cannot go ahead as described. This week, pick one free offline method, test it on scanned lease and fire-certificate PDFs, record the accuracy, and wire a confirmed date into the reminder engine so the whole chain runs on the demo laptop. Write down how many weeks you have, since the record does not say, and keep the highlight as the centrepiece only once those numbers exist.
